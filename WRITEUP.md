# Blackout Beacon — Submission

When a storm takes down the power grid, it takes every cloud AI with it: at the exact moment someone needs to ask whether their insulin survived six hours in a warm fridge, ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are all on the wrong side of a broken cable. Blackout Beacon turns one MacBook into a neighbourhood lifeline. The laptop broadcasts its own WiFi network, BEACON; any phone joins with no app, no account and no internet, opens a browser at http://beacon.help, and gets a calm, multilingual emergency assistant that streams grounded answers, with a running counter reading "Bytes sent to the internet: 0".

The architecture is a deliberate hybrid, because the two halves of the problem want different models. Small offline models are dependable but do not reliably know your local emergency numbers or your water company's boil-notice procedure; frontier cloud models know all of it but need a connection. So before the blackout, briefing.py uses Gemini 3.7 Flash through the official google-genai SDK with structured outputs to compile 12-15 location-tailored JSON readiness cards: UK emergency numbers, first aid, water safety, power-cut guidance, nearby hospitals, multilingual phrasebooks. During the blackout, Gemma 4 (e4b) runs fully locally via Ollama and grounds every answer in those cards; Gemini's knowledge is distilled into files while the internet exists, and Gemma carries it when it does not. The serving layer is built to match the setting: beacon_server.py is Python stdlib only, zero dependencies, because a disaster tool must not have a supply chain, and it runs a wildcard DNS server that answers every query with the laptop's address. That captive DNS choice is what makes the product feel inevitable on a phone: users do not install anything or learn an IP address, they type any domain they can remember and land on the beacon, with an SSE-streamed chat and a fair one-at-a-time queue sharing one laptop's inference across every phone in range. Getting there produced real engineering scars, documented in the README's field notes: macOS 26 refusing to share from a link-less interface (solved with a feth fake-cable), and Samsung's HTTPS-first connectivity probes suppressing captive popups (solved by making the popup optional rather than load-bearing).

The roadmap follows directly from what one beacon proves. A mesh of beacons, laptops and Raspberry Pis sharing cards over ad-hoc links, turns one WiFi cell into street-scale coverage; the stdlib-only server ports to a Pi essentially unchanged. Pre-briefed regional card packs, compiled centrally while the internet is healthy, would make a beacon useful even to an owner who never ran the briefing step, and solar and battery power profiles extend hours into days. The premise stays the same throughout: the cloud teaches the laptop before the storm, and the laptop carries the neighbourhood through it.
